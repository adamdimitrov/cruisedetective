London Tourism Statistics 2026 (How Many Tourists Visit?)

By Adam Dimitrov, Owner & Head Cruise Detective
Compiled from Official ONS & VisitBritain Data • 6 Charts with Data Tables • Methodology
Last updated: Nov 13, 2026

London welcomed an estimated 22.0 million overseas visits and 123.8 million visitor nights in 2024, fully regaining pre-pandemic volume according to the Office for National Statistics (ONS) International Passenger Survey.

This report compiles official London tourism statistics from the ONS, VisitBritain, the Greater London Authority (London Datastore), and Heathrow Airport: inbound visits, domestic tourism trips, length of stay, leading country markets, total expenditure, and aviation traffic figures. All charts include verifiable data tables and primary reference sources.

How many tourists visit London each year?

London welcomed approximately 22.0 million international visits in 2024, up 8.5% from 20.28 million in 2023. International visitor volume has now fully recovered past its pre-pandemic high of 21.71 million recorded in 2019.

The International Passenger Survey (IPS) is conducted continuously by the ONS at all major UK airports, seaports, and the Eurostar terminal. Following severe disruption during 2020 and 2021 when international borders were restricted, London recaptured its role as the primary UK gateway destination.

London captures more than half of all overseas visits to the United Kingdom (53.4% in 2023 and 53.8% in 2024). Holiday travel accounts for 53% of visits to London, followed by visits to friends and relatives (VFR) at 27%, and business trips at 16%.

Unlike continental European city destinations such as Paris or Amsterdam where visitor statistics are derived from hotel registry forms, London's official tourism metrics come from traveler surveys. As a result, ONS figures capture all accommodation types: commercial hotels, short-stay holiday apartments, hostels, and nights spent staying with friends and relatives.

Total overnight volume reached 123.8 million in 2024, demonstrating that travelers are making slightly longer trips than before 2020. The expansion of remote working and combined "bleisure" trips (combining business travel with weekend leisure stays) has supported sustained room night volumes.

London's average stay of 5.63 nights is substantially longer than that of Paris (2.27 hotel nights) or Amsterdam (2.33 hotel nights) because the UK figures encompass all forms of accommodation and reflect London's status as a major global hub for long-haul vacationers and family reunions.

Duration varies sharply by purpose of visit. Holidaymakers stay an average of 4.5 nights, business travelers stay 3.8 nights, and travelers visiting friends or relatives average 8.4 nights in the capital.

Where do visitors to London come from?

The United States is London's largest international market, generating 3.40 million visits (16.8% of all overseas arrivals), followed by France (1.80 million), Germany (1.50 million), and the Republic of Ireland (1.20 million).

The United States represents London's most valuable overseas tourism market. American visitors account for roughly one in six overseas trips to the city and generate more than 22% of total inbound visitor expenditure. Nearly half of all nights spent by Americans visiting the UK are spent in London.

Close European neighbours (France, Germany, Ireland, Spain, and Italy) generate a steady volume of city breaks and cultural getaways throughout the year, bolstered by direct flights and high-frequency Eurostar train services into St Pancras International.

How much do tourists spend in London?

Inbound overseas visitors spent an estimated £18.0 billion in London in 2024, expanding 7.8% over 2023 (£16.70 billion) and 14.6% above the 2019 pre-pandemic baseline (£15.70 billion).

How important is domestic tourism to London?

British residents took 15.9 million domestic overnight trips to London in 2023, while domestic day visitors made an additional 217 million tourism day visits generating £14.8 billion in spending.

According to VisitBritain's Great Britain Tourism Survey (GBTS), London's share of all domestic overnight trips in England rose from 15% in 2022 to 16% in 2023 and 17% in 2024, demonstrating renewed preference for urban cultural city breaks.

Domestic day trips represent massive economic volume for London's leisure sector. In 2023, visitors living in the South East, East of England, and the Home Counties made 217 million leisure day visits into London (+24% year-on-year), contributing 14.8 billion pounds in retail, dining, and attraction spending.

How busy is London Heathrow Airport?

London Heathrow Airport processed 83.86 million passengers in 2024, setting an all-time annual record with a 5.9% increase over 2023 (79.15 million) and surpassing its pre-pandemic 2019 record of 80.89 million.

Heathrow stands as the busiest airport in Europe and the primary hub for British Airways and the oneworld alliance. Transatlantic capacity between London and North American gateways expanded significantly in 2024, driving the airport to its busiest operational year in history.

Across the wider London airport system (comprising Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Luton, and London City), total passenger volume exceeded 165 million travelers in 2024, maintaining London's status as the busiest city airspace system in the world.

What are the top destinations to visit in London?

London features an extraordinary collection of historic Royal palaces, national museums, and UNESCO heritage sites. Five of the most popular attractions include:

  • The British Museum: Dedicated to human history, art, and culture, the Bloomsbury institution welcomes over 5.8 million visitors annually, housing treasures such as the Rosetta Stone and the Parthenon Sculptures.
  • The Tower of London: Founded by William the Conqueror in 1066, this historic fortress and UNESCO World Heritage site houses the Crown Jewels and attracts approximately 2.8 million visitors per year.
  • The River Thames and Waterfront Landmarks: The Thames flows past London's most historic sights, from the Houses of Parliament and Big Ben to Tower Bridge and Greenwich. Booking an undercover-tested Thames river cruise between Westminster Pier and Greenwich provides the best vantage point for photographing the South Bank, the London Eye, and St Paul's Cathedral.
  • Tate Modern: Located in the repurposed Bankside Power Station on the south bank of the Thames, Britain's national museum of modern and contemporary art welcomes roughly 4.7 million visitors annually.
  • The National Gallery: Overlooking Trafalgar Square, this premier institution houses more than 2,300 Western European paintings dating from the mid-13th century to 1900, welcoming over 3.1 million visitors each year.

Sources and methodology

  • Inbound International Visits, Nights and Expenditure: Sourced from the Office for National Statistics (ONS), International Passenger Survey (IPS) and annual Travel trends publications (2019 to 2024). Regional breakdown tables for London retrieved via the Greater London Authority (London Datastore). Data accessed November 2026. Note that the United Kingdom is not included in Eurostat datasets after 2020.
  • Domestic Tourism and Day Visits: Sourced from VisitBritain, Great Britain Tourism Survey (GBTS) for domestic overnight trips and Great Britain Day Visits Survey (GBDVS) for leisure day visits (2022, 2023, 2024). GBTS figures from 2022 onward represent official statistics in development under new survey methodology.
  • Heathrow Airport Statistics: Sourced from Heathrow Airport Limited investor centre traffic reports and annual passenger traffic releases (2019 to 2024).
  • Definitions: An inbound visit represents an international trip by an overseas resident that involves at least one night spent in London. Length of stay is calculated as total overseas visitor nights divided by overseas visits. Expenditure represents total spending in London by foreign visitors during their stay, excluding international airfares.
