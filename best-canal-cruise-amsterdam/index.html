Every Amsterdam canal cruise looks the same online: the same canal ring, thousands of good reviews and an hour or so on the water. The real differences are the boat, who tells the stories, and how warm you stay. So during my 7 days in Amsterdam I booked the 5 most popular canal cruises myself, in November, rain included.

I judged the boat size and comfort, the commentary, the snacks and drinks, how each boat handled the cold, and where it leaves from. Below is the short version: which one won, why, and when one of the other four is the better pick for you. The full reviews and photos are in my complete Amsterdam canal cruise ranking.

Why Captain Jack Took the #1 Spot

The 5 cruises split into two kinds: big covered boats with a recorded audio guide (Lover's Canal, Blue Boat) and small boats with a live skipper (Captain Jack, Flagship, Friendship). The small boats were more fun, and Captain Jack did everything that matters a little better than the other two: the best storyteller, the widest snack and drink selection, the most comfortable boat, and a longer 90-minute route. Here is what I found on board.

1. A Real Captain, Not a Recording

My skipper was an older, old-school sailor who fit the role perfectly. He was a great storyteller with a deep voice and a relaxed pace, and he was happy to answer every question we had. On the big boats you listen to a recording through earbuds. On Blue Boat the skipper didn't even introduce himself.

2. The Best Unlimited Snacks and Drinks

I tried several "unlimited food and drinks" cruises, and this one had the most extensive selection of all: stroopwafel, Christmas ginger cookies, Gouda cheese, sausage and crackers, plus soft drinks, wines and beers. Flagship only had Gouda, and Friendship served no food at all. If you don't want the drinks package, there is a classic cruise with optional drinks too.

3. A Small, Comfortable Boat That Opens Up in Summer

The boat takes up to 20 passengers, so it never feels crowded, and the seats are padded with pillows. In winter it is insulated but not heated. That meant no fogged-up windows, and I did not feel cold in November. In summer the sidewalls and roof come off and the boat becomes fully open-air.

4. A Longer Route, Two Piers

At 90 minutes it was the longest of the five (the others run 60 to 75 minutes), and it loops the canal ring counterclockwise, the opposite way to most boats, which I enjoyed. You can board near Central Station or near the Rijksmuseum.

What I Didn't Love

  • It is not heated. I was fine in November, but on a freezing day the heated big boats (Lover's Canal, Blue Boat) are the safer pick.
  • No toilet on board. Go before you board, since the cruise lasts 90 minutes.
  • Not wheelchair accessible. Blue Boat has a wheelchair boat on request.
  • Seats are first come, first served, like on nearly every Amsterdam canal cruise. Arrive a little early if you want a window seat.

Quick Questions

Should I book the unlimited snacks and drinks option?

If you like to snack and sip, yes. It is the reason Captain Jack won: it had the widest selection of the 5 cruises I tested. If you only want the views, note that every Captain Jack cruise on the FareHarbor page we link to includes the drinks and snacks: from €25 for the evening cruise and €39.95 for daytime cruises, all from the Rijksmuseum.

When does it sail?

When I checked for Saturday 14 November 2026, the all-inclusive cruises left the Rijksmuseum dock at 11:00, 13:00, 15:00, 17:00 and 19:00, with sightseeing-only cruises at 14:00 and 16:00. Times change by season, so check the booking page for your date.

Is it OK in winter or in the rain?

The boat is insulated, not heated. I did not feel cold in November. If the forecast is freezing, Lover's Canal and Blue Boat are heated.

Where exactly do I board?

There are two meeting points: Prins Hendrikkade 33b near Central Station, and Museumbrug 2 near the Rijksmuseum. Check your ticket for which one you booked.

Can I bring kids?

Yes. Infants up to 4 ride free, and the boat is stroller accessible.

My pick for your Amsterdam canal cruise

Captain Jack with the unlimited snacks and drinks option, from Central Station or the Rijksmuseum. Go in daylight if the sights are the point.
