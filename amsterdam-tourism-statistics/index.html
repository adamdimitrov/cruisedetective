Amsterdam Tourism Statistics 2026 (How Many Tourists Visit?)

By Adam Dimitrov, Owner & Head Cruise Detective
Compiled from Official CBS StatLine Data • 6 Charts with Data Tables • Methodology
Last updated: Nov 13, 2026

Amsterdam recorded 9.55 million hotel guests and 22.20 million hotel nights in 2025, according to official figures from Statistics Netherlands (CBS). International visitors generated 80.7% of all hotel guests and 84.7% of overnight stays.

This report compiles official Amsterdam tourism statistics from CBS StatLine, the City of Amsterdam Research and Statistics bureau (OIS), and Royal Schiphol Group: annual arrivals, overnight stays, length of stay, monthly seasonality, leading source markets, tourist tax receipts, and airport passenger volumes. Every chart is paired with a verifiable data table.

How many tourists visit Amsterdam each year?

Amsterdam hotels welcomed 9.55 million guests in 2025, an increase of 2.6% from 9.31 million in 2024. Total hotel guest numbers have exceeded their 2019 pre-pandemic volume (9.21 million) for two consecutive years.

CBS tracks hotel guests across hotels, motels, boarding houses, and youth accommodation facilities with at least 5 bed spaces within the municipality of Amsterdam (GM0363). A traveler checking into multiple hotels during their stay is registered at each establishment.

During the pandemic low of 2021, total guest numbers dropped to 2.89 million. By 2025, international guest volume reached 7.70 million, fully recovering from border restrictions and setting an all-time record for foreign visitation to Amsterdam.

How many nights do tourists spend in Amsterdam?

Total overnight stays have grown substantially faster than arrivals, reflecting a lengthening duration of stay among international visitors. International guests generated 84.7% of all hotel room nights in 2025 (18.81 million nights), cementing Amsterdam as one of the most internationally dominated city destinations in Europe.

Dutch domestic travelers contributed 3.39 million nights in 2025 (15.3% of the municipal total), down slightly from 3.57 million nights in 2024 as higher room rates and hotel taxes led some regional domestic travelers to choose short-stay alternatives outside the A10 ring road.

The average duration of stay in Amsterdam hotels has steadily increased by 16.5% compared to 2019 (from 2.00 nights to 2.33 nights). Foreign visitors stay roughly one-third longer on average (2.44 nights) than Dutch domestic travelers (1.84 nights).

Intercontinental travelers from long-haul markets record the longest hotel durations in Amsterdam: American guests averaged 2.48 nights in 2025, Canadian guests averaged 2.61 nights, and Australian guests averaged 2.68 nights.

Amsterdam exhibits a major spring peak in April and May, coinciding with the world-famous Dutch tulip bloom season, Keukenhof gardens opening, and King's Day (Koningsdag) celebrations on April 27. In 2025, April hotel nights reached 2.11 million, expanding 10.0% over April 2024.

Summer visitation peaks in July and August (2.22 million nights in August 2025), before autumn cultural events, the Amsterdam Dance Event (ADE) in October (1.97 million nights), and Amsterdam Light Festival in December provide sustained volume through year-end.

Unlike many national statistics offices that only publish country data for the country as a whole, Statistics Netherlands publishes origin country data directly for the Amsterdam municipality (GM0363). The United States and the United Kingdom are the dominant foreign markets, together accounting for 5.59 million hotel nights (29.7% of all foreign nights in Amsterdam).

Long-haul travel demonstrated rapid growth in 2025: Canadian visitor nights surged 20.8% (to 418,000), American nights expanded 14.0% (to 2.82 million), and Australian nights grew 9.4% (to 442,000).

How much do tourists spend in Amsterdam?

The City of Amsterdam budgeted approximately 242.5 million euros in tourist tax revenues in 2024, after raising its tourist tax rate to 12.5%. Across the Netherlands as a whole, total tourism expenditure reached 111.2 billion euros in 2024 according to CBS.

In 2024, the City Council of Amsterdam enacted an increase in the municipal tourist tax (toeristenbelasting) to 12.5% of the net accommodation price, creating the highest tourist tax rate in the European Union. This policy was designed to generate municipal revenue to fund public services and manage visitor pressure in historic central neighbourhoods.

At the national level, CBS Tourism Satellite Accounts report that total tourism spending in the Netherlands reached 111.2 billion euros in 2024, with foreign travelers contributing 38.2 billion euros. Amsterdam accounts for the largest municipal share of inbound visitor expenditure in the country.

Amsterdam Airport Schiphol operates as one of Europe's top four international hub airports and the global headquarters of KLM Royal Dutch Airlines. In 2024, 45.3 million passengers travelled to or from European destinations, while 21.5 million were intercontinental travelers.

Across the wider Royal Schiphol Group (which includes regional hubs Eindhoven Airport and Rotterdam The Hague Airport), total passenger volume reached 75.9 million travelers in 2024 on 529,248 flight movements.

What are the top destinations to visit in Amsterdam?

Amsterdam offers an exceptional concentration of 17th-century cultural heritage and art museums. Five of the most visited sights include:

  • Rijksmuseum: The national museum of the Netherlands welcoming over 2.7 million visitors annually, showcasing Dutch Golden Age masterpieces including Rembrandt's The Night Watch and Vermeer's The Milkmaid.
  • Van Gogh Museum: Housing the largest collection of Vincent van Gogh's artwork in the world, including 200 paintings and 500 drawings, welcoming over 2.2 million visitors each year.
  • The 17th-Century Canal Ring (Grachtengordel): Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2010, the historic network of concentric waterways (Herengracht, Keizersgracht, and Prinsengracht) is the city's defining feature. Exploring by an undercover-reviewed Amsterdam canal cruise on an open electric sloop or classic salon boat is the quintessential visitor experience.
  • Anne Frank House: Located on the Prinsengracht canal, the historic biographical museum preserving the secret annex where Anne Frank hid during World War II receives roughly 1.2 million visitors annually.
  • Vondelpark: The city's most celebrated 47-hectare public park, welcoming over 10 million visits annually from residents and travelers for walking, cycling, and open-air concerts.

Sources and methodology

  • Tourist Tax and Local Municipal Finance: Sourced from Gemeente Amsterdam, Directie Financiën and Onderzoek en Statistiek (OIS), municipal budget and actual tax revenue reports (2023, 2024).
  • National Tourism Expenditure: Sourced from CBS Tourism Satellite Accounts (Toeristische bestedingen in Nederland, 2023 and 2024).
  • Airport Traffic Statistics: Sourced from Royal Schiphol Group annual traffic reviews and operational statistics for Amsterdam Airport Schiphol (2019 to 2024).
  • Definitions: CBS figures cover hotels, motels, boarding houses, hotel-apartments, youth accommodation, and bed & breakfasts with a capacity of at least 5 beds. Private short-term holiday rentals (e.g. Airbnb) and day visits are excluded from the hotel series. Duration of stay is computed as total overnight stays divided by registered hotel guests.
