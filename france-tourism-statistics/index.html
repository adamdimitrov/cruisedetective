France Tourism Statistics 2026 (How Many Tourists Visit?)

By Adam Dimitrov, Owner & Head Cruise Detective
Compiled from Official Eurostat Data • 8 Charts with Data Tables • Methodology
Last updated: Nov 14, 2026

France recorded 189.31 million check-ins at tourist accommodation and 471.65 million nights in hotels, holiday rentals and campsites in 2025, more than in any year since at least 2019. International guests accounted for 31.0% of arrivals and spent €77.5 billion in the country.

A check-in is not a person: the same traveller is counted every time they check in, and French residents on holiday at home make up most of the total. This report covers tourism in France as a whole: how many tourists visit, how long they stay, which months and regions are busiest, where visitors come from and how much they spend. For the capital only, see our Paris tourism statistics. Every chart has a data table and a link to its official source.

  • France recorded 189,312,165 check-ins at tourist accommodation in 2025, 4.5% more than in 2024 and 8.4% above 2019.
  • Guests spent 471,652,349 nights in French hotels, holiday rentals and campsites, the highest figure since at least 2019 and 5.6% above that year.
  • International guests made 58.74 million arrivals (31.0%) and spent 150.76 million nights (32.0%). Foreign nights grew 7.2% in a year, more than twice as fast as the total.
  • August is the peak month with 91.23 million nights, almost five times as many as November (18.50 million). July and August together account for 36.0% of the year.
  • Germany is the largest foreign market with 23.19 million nights, followed by the Netherlands (19.85 million) and the United Kingdom (18.31 million).
  • Île-de-France, the Paris region, takes 32.5% of all foreign nights in France, more than Provence-Alpes-Côte d’Azur and Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes combined.
  • International visitors spent €77.48 billion in France in 2025, 9.0% more than in 2024 and 36.6% more than in 2019 (not adjusted for inflation).
  • France ranks third in the EU for nights spent by foreign guests, behind Spain and Italy.

How many tourists visit France each year?

France recorded 189.31 million check-ins at hotels, holiday rentals and campsites in 2025, up 4.5% on 2024. Of these, 58.74 million were international guests, 7.6% more than a year earlier.

The French market is mostly domestic: residents of France made 69.0% of all arrivals. International arrivals took longer to recover after 2020, but by 2025 they were 13.9% above 2019, while domestic arrivals were 6.1% above.

These are arrivals at registered accommodation, not people. A visitor who moves between two hotels counts twice, and visitors staying with friends or family are not counted at all.

How does France compare with other EU countries?

France ranks third in the EU for nights spent by foreign guests in tourist accommodation, with 150.76 million in 2025, 9.8% of the EU total. Spain (328.93 million) and Italy (301.62 million) record about twice as many.

Sources and methodology

  • Arrivals, nights and length of stay: Eurostat, tour_occ_arnat (arrivals) and tour_occ_ninat (nights), NACE codes I551 to I553: hotels, holiday and other short-stay accommodation, and campsites. Length of stay is nights divided by arrivals. Data accessed 14 November 2026 (dataset updated 12 October 2026).
  • Months: Eurostat tour_occ_nim, 2025. Monthly figures are published separately from the annual series and can differ from the annual total by a small rounding or revision margin.
  • Countries of origin: Eurostat tour_occ_ninraw, nights by country of residence of the guest, 2024 and 2025.
  • Regions: Eurostat tour_occ_nin2, nights by NUTS region, 2025.
  • Spending: Eurostat bop_its6_det, balance of payments item SD (travel), credit, partner: rest of the world, EUR million. This is what non-residents spend in the country on goods and services during trips, including business travel and students, but excluding international passenger transport.
  • What the data does not cover: stays with friends and family, unregistered private rentals and day trips are not in the accommodation statistics. An arrival is a check-in, so one traveller staying at two hotels on the same trip counts twice. Eurostat publishes figures supplied by INSEE for France.
