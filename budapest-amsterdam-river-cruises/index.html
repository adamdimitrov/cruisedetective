By Adam Dimitrov, Owner & Head Cruise Detective
Compared on Nov 16, 2026 using live listings • Independent comparison
Last updated: Nov 16, 2026

  • Best all-inclusive: Scenic: Jewels of Europe, drinks all day, $5,244 in Feb
  • Best boutique: Uniworld: European Jewels, unlimited drinks incl. spirits

Is a Budapest to Amsterdam River Cruise Worth It?

Fifteen days, four countries and around 14 towns without packing your suitcase once. At $3,976 for Emerald's February sailing that is about $265 per person per day for your cabin, 41 meals, wine and beer at lunch and dinner, guided walks in nearly every port, airport transfers and tips. Booking the same hotels, trains and tours yourself across Hungary, Austria, Germany and the Netherlands would cost about the same and take a lot more planning.

The Route: What You See in 15 Days

Every cruise here follows the same three rivers. The Danube from Budapest through Bratislava and Vienna, the Wachau Valley (Dürnstein and Melk) and Passau to Regensburg; the Main-Danube Canal over the European watershed to Nuremberg and Bamberg; the Main through Würzburg and Miltenberg; and the Rhine through the Rhine Gorge, past Rüdesheim and Koblenz to Cologne and Amsterdam.

The differences are small but real: Uniworld adds Heidelberg and Rothenburg, Scenic adds Bonn, Lueftner stops in Utrecht, and Emerald and Avalon include Bratislava. The bigger decision is the direction, and it is not about the current. The ship passes about 68 locks and crosses the European watershed at 406 m above sea level, so it sails upstream for half the trip whichever way you go, because the Rhine flows north to the sea and the Danube flows east. So pick by your flights and your extra days: start in Amsterdam if you want to finish with a few days in Budapest, or the other way round if you would rather end with the canals. If you plan extra nights in Amsterdam, the Amsterdam tourism statistics show which months the city is busiest.

Budapest to Amsterdam River Cruises: FAQ

How much does a Budapest to Amsterdam river cruise cost?

For 15 days, expect from about $3,200 to $4,000 per person on Emerald or Lueftner on their cheapest dates, $5,200 to $7,500 on Scenic, Viking and Avalon, and $8,400 to $9,000 on Uniworld. The same cruise often costs twice as much in May as in late February, so the date matters more than the line.

How long is a river cruise from Budapest to Amsterdam?

The standard cruise is 15 days with 14 nights on board, sailing about 1,100 miles along the Danube, the Main-Danube Canal, the Main and the Rhine. Some lines also sell 17 to 22-day versions that add hotel nights in Budapest or Amsterdam or a Belgian extension.

Which river cruise line is best for Budapest to Amsterdam?

For value, Emerald Cruises: from $3,976 with tips, transfers and wine at dinner included. For everything included, Scenic (drinks all day, tips and transfers) or Uniworld (unlimited drinks including spirits and tips). For the cabin, Avalon's Panorama Suites. Lueftner has the best operator rating at 4.5 from 220 reviews.

Is it better to sail Budapest to Amsterdam or Amsterdam to Budapest?

The stops are the same either way, so pick the direction by price and by where you want to spend extra days. Neither way follows the current the whole trip: the Rhine flows north to the North Sea and the Danube flows east toward the Black Sea, so from Amsterdam you sail upstream on the Rhine and Main and downstream on the Danube, and from Budapest the reverse. Lines sell both directions on alternating dates, and prices differ by date, not direction: Emerald's cheapest sailing on this route goes Amsterdam to Budapest on 20 February 2027 for $3,163.

Are drinks and tips included on a Budapest to Amsterdam river cruise?

It depends on the line. Scenic and Uniworld include drinks all day and tips. Emerald includes tips and wine, beer and soft drinks with lunch and dinner. Lueftner, Avalon and Viking include drinks with meals but not tips, so add those to the fare when comparing.

When is the best time for a Budapest to Amsterdam river cruise?

April to June and September to October for mild weather and green vineyards; late November and December for the Christmas markets; late February and early March for the lowest prices. In very dry late summers, low water on the Danube or Rhine can force a ship swap or a coach section, so travel insurance is worth having.
