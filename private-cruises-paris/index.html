Prices are per boat, not per person, and none include hotel pickup, so I also checked where each one actually leaves from.

🏆 #1 Best Overall Private Cruise
Visite Paris En Bateau • ⏱️ 1.5 Hours • Port de Javel Haut

Private Seine Cruise with Champagne on a Small Boat

5.0(410 reviews)
From €399
▾

Pros:

  • 410 reviews, 5.0/5: the most-reviewed private boat in Paris on GetYourGuide.
  • Cheapest private boat: €399 for up to 4, Champagne and water included.
  • Larger boat for 5 to 8: €520 for 6, €660 for 8, with a table for your own picnic.

Summary & Review Overview ▾

This is the private cruise with by far the most reviews in Paris: 410 on GetYourGuide with a 5.0/5 average. It is a small family business, and reviewers keep naming their captain and saying they took photos for them along the way.

You get the boat to yourselves for 1.5 hours from Port de Javel Haut, next to the small Statue of Liberty: past the Eiffel Tower, Pont Alexandre III, Musée d'Orsay, the Louvre and Pont Neuf to Notre-Dame and back. A bottle of Champagne and water are included, there is a Bluetooth speaker for your music, and you can bring your own food and drinks.

There are two boats. The Standard boat is €399 for 1 to 4 people and has no table. For bigger groups the Luxury boat takes up to 8: €520 for 5 or 6 people, €590 for 7 and €660 for 8. It is a little larger and more comfortable, and has a table. The operator says the service is the same on both.

💡 Tip: the captain can drop you near the Louvre at the end instead of back at Javel. Ask when you board if you want to continue your evening in the centre.

Same pier as #1 but a different feel: a comfortable boat that seats 10, limited to 6 passengers, with rosé, Evian water and macarons served during the cruise. It has 155 reviews at 5.0/5, and they read like a list of special occasions: proposals, wedding anniversaries, family trips.

The route runs from Port de Javel Haut to the Eiffel Tower for a 10-minute photo stop, then past the Grand Palais, Pont Alexandre III, Musée d'Orsay and the Louvre to Notre-Dame, and back on the other side of the river. The evening cruise leaves at 20:00. In summer it can finish before dark, which leaves time for dinner afterwards. Children are welcome and there are life jackets in all sizes.

💡 Tip for proposals: mention it in the booking notes. One reviewer wrote that the captain knew exactly when to give them privacy at the Eiffel Tower.

Is a Private Seine Cruise Worth It?

A shared sightseeing cruise costs about €17 to €25 per person for an hour on a boat with a few hundred other people (I compared them in my Seine sightseeing cruise guide, and this is the one I'd book). A private boat costs €399 to €660 for the whole boat, so it is a splurge for 2 people but surprisingly reasonable for a group: €490 split 6 ways is about €82 each.

What you get for that: the boat to yourselves, your own music, your own Champagne or picnic on most boats, a captain who stops where you want photos, and a route that goes right past the Eiffel Tower, the Louvre and Notre-Dame without a loudspeaker commentary. If you want dinner served on board as well, a shared dinner cruise is still the better deal (here is my pick after testing the top 5).

Where Do Private Cruises in Paris Leave From?

The pier matters more than it looks, because none of these cruises include hotel pickup:

  • Port de Javel Haut, 15th arrondissement (#1, #2, and #4 nearby): by the small Statue of Liberty replica, a few minutes by boat from the Eiffel Tower. Nearest stations: Javel (RER C) and Javel-André Citroën (Métro 10).
  • Issy-les-Moulineaux (#3): just outside the city limit to the south-west. Allow extra travel time.
  • Quai François Mitterrand, 1st arrondissement (#5 and #6): a short walk from the Louvre, the most central of all.

Captains often message you before the cruise to confirm the spot. Save their number, because the small boats don't have a ticket office or a sign.

Best Time for a Private Cruise

Book the departure that ends after sunset. After dark the Eiffel Tower sparkles for 5 minutes at the start of every hour, and a good captain will time the turn so you are right in front of it. In summer that means a late departure (sunset is after 21:30 in June); in winter you can see the lights on a late-afternoon cruise.

Small private boats are open to the weather. On a cold evening ask whether blankets are on board, or pick the Paris Water Way VIP boat, which is heated and has a roof.

Private Cruises in Paris: FAQ

How much does a private Seine cruise in Paris cost?

Private boats you can book online cost €399 for up to 4 people and €480 to €660 for groups of 6 to 8 for 1.5 hours, and about €1,250 for a boat that takes up to 12. The price is per boat, so a full boat of 6 at €490 works out at about €82 per person.

Can I bring my own food and Champagne on a private boat?

On most small private boats, yes: Visite Paris En Bateau, Boat in Paris, MyParisRiver and the Paris Water Way pontoon all allow your own food and drinks. The Paris Water Way VIP boat does not; it sells drinks and food boards on board instead.

Which private cruise is best for a proposal?

Boat in Paris (rosé and macarons, a 10-minute photo stop at the Eiffel Tower) or Visite Paris En Bateau (a bottle of Champagne included). Book an evening departure and mention the proposal in the booking notes so the captain can plan the moment.

Is there a toilet on private Seine boats?

Only on the Paris Water Way VIP boat for up to 12. The small private boats have no toilet, which is one reason they last about 1.5 hours.

Is hotel pickup included?

No. None of these listings include hotel pickup: you meet the boat at its pier. Two piers are in Port de Javel in the 15th arrondissement, one is in Issy-les-Moulineaux, and Paris Water Way leaves from the 1st, near the Louvre.

What happens if it rains?

Private boats run in light rain, and some have a canopy or a roof; the Paris Water Way VIP boat has a convertible roof and heating. If the operator cancels for bad weather you can move to a new date or get a full refund, and you can cancel for free up to 24 hours before.

How I Picked These 6 Private Cruises

On Nov 13, 2026 I searched every private Seine boat bookable on GetYourGuide and Tiqets (Tiqets currently sells none). I kept boats with a rating of 4.8 or higher and at least 15 reviews, then picked the one that does each job best: overall value, couples, the longest cruise, the best guide, the most central pier and the largest group. Prices, ratings, inclusions and piers are taken from the live listings on that date and can change.

Several operators sail almost identical small boats from the same pier, so I left out near-duplicates with fewer reviews rather than padding the list.
