2026 Sydney Dinner Cruise Comparison

The 7 Best Sydney Harbour Dinner Cruises (2026)

By Adam Dimitrov, Owner & Head Cruise Detective
Independent side-by-side comparison
Last updated: Nov 20, 2026

The right Sydney Harbour dinner cruise is the one that fits your night: a 4- or 5-course tasting menu under the Opera House, dinner with the wine and beer already paid, a cabaret show on a paddle steamer, or a twilight sail on a timber tall ship. Only one of the seven includes wine and beer, so the ticket is rarely the price of your evening; the menu, the drinks and the wharf are what separate them. This page gets you to that boat in under a minute.

To make the choice easy, I compared Sydney's most popular harbour dinner cruises side by side on what decides it: the menu and number of courses, which drinks are included, the show, the wharf, the length and the price per person, checked on each seller's own page. Is a Sydney Harbour Dinner Cruise Worth It?

Yes for a special night: you eat with the Opera House and Harbour Bridge outside the window, which no restaurant in the city can match from the water. Tickets run from €48 for a buffet to €106 for dinner with a cabaret.

Budget for drinks on top: only the all-inclusive cruise (#2) has wine and beer in the price.

More boat trips compared side by side: Seine dinner cruises in Paris, Bosphorus dinner cruises in Istanbul, Nile dinner cruises in Cairo and Ha Long Bay cruises.

How to Choose Between Them

For the food, Captain Cook (#1) has the most menus up to a 5-course tasting, and the Glass Boat (#4) a 4-course menu with king prawns. For drinks in the price, #2. For a show, the cabaret paddle steamer (#3) or the Latin party boat (#7). For the experience, the tall ship (#5). For the price, the €48 buffet (#6).

Most boats leave from King Street Wharf in Darling Harbour; the buffet leaves from Circular Quay and the tall ship from Campbell's Cove in The Rocks.

What to Wear and When to Go

Only Captain Cook lists a dress code (smart casual); on the others, neat casual is fine. On the tall ship, wear flat shoes.

For daylight views on the way out and city lights on the way back, book a sunset departure. Sunset is around 19:30 to 19:50 in summer (December to February) and around 17:00 to 17:40 in winter (June to August).

Sydney Harbour Dinner Cruises: FAQ

How much is a Sydney Harbour dinner cruise?

From €48 (buffet) and €66.17 (tall ship) to €98 with drinks included and €106 with a cabaret show. Most exclude drinks.

Which Sydney dinner cruise is best?

Captain Cook's MV Sydney 2000 for most people; the all-inclusive cruise for drinks in the price (4.8, the best rating); the paddle steamer for a show.

Are drinks included on Sydney dinner cruises?

Only on the all-inclusive cruise (NSW wines, beer, soft drinks). On the others you buy them at the bar.

Where do Sydney dinner cruises leave from?

Mostly King Street Wharf in Darling Harbour; also Circular Quay and Campbell's Cove in The Rocks.

Is there a dress code?

Captain Cook asks for smart casual; the others list none.

Are there vegetarian options?

Yes on Captain Cook, the cabaret cruise (full vegetarian menu), the Glass Boat (vegan menu) and the tall ship (vegetarian main).
